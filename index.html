02 / How it works

Seven stages, each with a different job.

Each analyst sees only its own data and writes its own view. The researchers then argue from those views, and nothing reaches the final ruling until the risk manager has reviewed it.

#StageAnalyst viewIn plain English
01FundamentalsAnalystRevenue and EPS trend, margins, FCF, leverage, valuation multiples vs historyIs the business actually making money, and is the price fair?
02SentimentAnalystSocial and retail positioning, insider transactions, crowd momentumWhat are other investors feeling and doing?
03News & macroAnalystCompany events, guidance, regulation, rates and sector catalystsWhat's happening in the world that could move this stock?
04TechnicalsAnalystTrend vs 50/200-day averages, MACD, RSI, volume, support and resistanceWhat is the price chart saying right now?
05Bull vs bearResearchers · debateMulti-round adversarial rebuttal built on stages 01–04 and your thesisOne argues to buy, one argues not to. They have to answer each other.
06Risk reviewTrader + risk managerStop loss, take profit and position size relative to volatility and event riskIf you do buy, how much, and where do you get out?
07RulingPortfolio managerBUY / HOLD / SELL with written rationale on which side carried the debateThe final call, and exactly why.
03 / Thesis stress-test

Tell it why you want to buy. It tries to prove you wrong.

Add a sentence about your reasoning and the bear researchers attack that exact premise. Page one of your report says whether your idea holds up.

  1. Write your reason in one line. "Safe dividend," "AI demand keeps growing," anything.
  2. The bear goes after it with the company's own numbers.
  3. You get a ruling on your thesis, its weakest assumption, and the conditions that would make you wrong.
