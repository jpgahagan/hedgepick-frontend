02 / How it works

Seven stages, each with a different job.

Each analyst sees only its own data and writes its own view. The researchers then argue from those views, and nothing reaches the final ruling until the risk manager has reviewed it.

01

Fundamentals

Analyst
Is the business actually making money, and is the price fair?Revenue and EPS trend, margins, FCF, leverage, valuation multiples vs history
02

Sentiment

Analyst
What are other investors feeling and doing?Social and retail positioning, insider transactions, crowd momentum
03

News & macro

Analyst
What's happening in the world that could move this stock?Company events, guidance, regulation, rates and sector catalysts
04

Technicals

Analyst
What is the price chart saying right now?Trend vs 50/200-day averages, MACD, RSI, volume, support and resistance
05

Bull vs bear

Researchers · debate
One argues to buy, one argues not to. They have to answer each other.Multi-round adversarial rebuttal built on stages 01–04 and your thesis
06

Risk review

Trader + risk manager
If you do buy, how much, and where do you get out?Stop loss, take profit and position size relative to volatility and event risk
07

Ruling

Portfolio manager
The final call, and exactly why.BUY / HOLD / SELL with written rationale on which side carried the debate
03 / Thesis stress-test

Tell it why you want to buy. It tries to prove you wrong.

Add a sentence about your reasoning and the bear researchers attack that exact premise. Page one of your report says whether your idea holds up.

  1. Write your reason in one line. "Safe dividend," "AI demand keeps growing," anything.
  2. The bear goes after it with the company's own numbers.
  3. You get a ruling on your thesis, its weakest assumption, and the conditions that would make you wrong.
Your thesis, tested · KO (Coca-Cola)Full sample PDF →
Your reasoning
"Defensive consumer staple with pricing power and a safe 3% dividend if the economy slows."
The bear went after this premise first.

Verdict on your thesis: partly holds. The dividend is well covered, but organic volume growth is negative (-1% in North America) as cumulative price hikes hit elasticity limits, and at 23x trailing earnings you are paying a growth multiple for a bond proxy.

Weakest assumption
That pricing power is inexhaustible. Unit case volume has already stalled in developed markets as private-label substitution rises.
Kill criteria
Exit if North American unit case volume stays negative for two consecutive quarters or operating margin compresses below 28%.
